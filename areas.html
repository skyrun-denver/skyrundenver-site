Every city writes its own rules. We know them.

Short-term rental licensing, primary residence requirements, and lodging tax differ from one city line to the next. The evaluation starts by reading the rules that apply to your home.

  • DenverReviewed November 12, 2026

    A short-term rental license covers stays of 29 nights or fewer and requires the home to be the host's primary residence. Stays of 30 nights or more fall under the residential rental property license instead. City source. Start in Denver

  • ArvadaReviewed November 12, 2026

    A short-term rental permit and a city business license are both required. Under rules in effect since May 1, 2026, one permit is allowed per lot (the main home or an accessory unit, not both), occupancy is two per bedroom plus two, and a home may operate up to 240 days in any 365. City source. Start in Arvada

  • Wheat RidgeReviewed November 12, 2026

    Licensed since 2021. Owner occupied partial home rentals are not capped. Whole home rentals are capped at 2 percent of homes per council district, with waitlists in three of four districts, and a 10 percent lodgers' tax applies. City source. Start in Wheat Ridge

  • LakewoodReviewed November 12, 2026

    Licensed since May 2023 and limited to the owner's primary residence, with proof of residence, $500,000 liability coverage with a short-term rental endorsement, a third party inspection, and certified notice to neighbors. $150 new, $75 renewal. City source. Start in Lakewood

  • EnglewoodReviewed November 12, 2026

    Stays under 30 nights need a license and an annual inspection, and the home must be an owner's primary residence, 185 days a year, unless the board grants a waiver. City sales tax of 3.8 percent and lodger's tax of 2 percent apply. City source. Start in Englewood

  • CentennialReviewed November 12, 2026

    Every short-term rental under 30 nights needs a city license, $150 a year plus a $50 application fee, liability insurance, two off street parking spaces, and occupancy of two per bedroom up to eight. The city pages state no primary residence requirement. City source. Start in Centennial

Rules summaries are verified against each city's current code and carry the date they were last reviewed. They are general information, not legal advice or a guarantee that a specific home qualifies.
